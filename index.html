A supplement stack is one ingredient paid for twice

A recovery stack gets judged by bottle count. Three bottles, three habits, done. The labels tell a different story: read them as one ledger and the stack usually pays for the same ingredient twice.

Take a common month: a pre-workout tub at $30, a recovery blend at $28, a sleep mix at $22. Eighty dollars a month, four distinct actives across the labels: caffeine, creatine, magnesium, theanine. On the bottle-count view that reads as three products doing three jobs.

The ledger view: the recovery blend carries creatine at 3 g a serving. The sleep mix carries a second copy of magnesium, which the blend already supplied. A mineral does not stack with itself; the second serving adds nothing the first did not cover. That $22 is partly a resupply of something already in the cart.

Dedup it: drop the blend, buy a plain creatine monohydrate tub at about $12 a month. New total: $64. Same four actives, $16 less every month, $192 a year. Price per active drops from $20 to $16, a 20 percent cut, and the bottle count never entered the argument.

Per gram the gap is starker. The blend serves 3 g a day, 90 g a month, $28: about 31 cents per gram. The tub serves 5 g a day, 150 g a month, $12: 8 cents per gram. The blend's creatine costs roughly four times the tub's, and it is not even the reason the blend was bought.

The method fits on an index card. One row per active ingredient: name, dose, which product carries it, its share of that product's price. A duplicate row is the audit finding: pay the first copy, cut the second. Cost per serving is a shelf number; cost per covered ingredient is the stack's real price.
